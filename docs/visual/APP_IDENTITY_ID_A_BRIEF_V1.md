# Daily Town ID-A — Sprout Town Mark Brief v1

Status: approved identity direction. Final icon/logo artwork requires visual candidate production and human lock.

## Core idea

Daily Town's core service mark is **not Moru-specific**. It represents a place that grows through walking, discovery and memory.

## Mark ingredients

Required visual ingredients:

- two-leaf sprout
- one subtle town/exploration cue chosen from path / doorway / small-house negative space
- compact geometric silhouette
- warm botanical tone

Avoid combining all cues at once if the mark becomes busy.

## Launcher icon goals

- recognisable at small Android launcher size
- single dominant silhouette
- survives monochrome/adaptive-icon treatment
- no tiny text
- no dependency on Moru face details
- sufficiently distinct from generic eco/plant icons through the path/town cue

## Candidate construction routes for artwork

### ID-A1 — Sprout + Path

Two leaves above a stem that becomes a winding path or route.

Strength: strongest exploration meaning; simplest geometry.
Risk: can resemble generic route/eco icons if path is too literal.

### ID-A2 — Sprout + Doorway — preferred first candidate

Two leaves sit above a simple doorway/arch/house-negative-space form.

Strength: strongest 'Town' and welcoming-home feeling while staying compact.
Risk: must avoid looking like a gardening/home-services logo.

### ID-A3 — Sprout + Town Tile

Sprout emerges from a rounded square/block that subtly reads as a neighborhood tile.

Strength: excellent launcher silhouette and system-icon compatibility.
Risk: exploration meaning is more implicit.

## Wordmark

`Daily Town`

Direction:

- friendly humanist sans or soft serif/sans pairing
- readable Korean/English coexistence
- not overly whimsical
- mark may appear left of wordmark or above for square lockup

## Relationship to Moru

Moru remains prominent in:

- onboarding
- store screenshots
- promotional/key art
- companion-focused notifications or event art

Moru is not required inside the permanent service mark.
